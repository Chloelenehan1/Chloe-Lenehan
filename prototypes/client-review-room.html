How it works

The rules the room holds to

One room per deliverable

Concept and cut live in the same room for each creator deliverable. Round 1, round 2 and round 3 stack as tabs. Earlier rounds stay readable, with each note marked as resolved or carried over and the creator's reply beside it.

Notes pin to the exact thing

On a concept or script, a note anchors to the words selected. On a cut, a note anchors to a second or a range of seconds, with a marker on the timeline and the line of transcript at that point.

The team sits between client and creator

Client notes go to the Fabulate team first. Odin drafts one consolidated change request and flags anything outside the contract, like an extra deliverable. A person sends it. The creator gets one clear brief, not five stakeholders.

No rubber-stamp approvals

The client sees what changed since the last round, Sentinel's compliance reading and every open note before they approve. Approving records their name, the round and the time. Requesting changes needs at least one note.

The portal shows the work, nothing else

The client sees published versions only: no rates, no internal notes, no agent chatter. The team's internal comments stay in the team view. This follows the guest access rules already designed.

Waiting is visible

Each item says who has the next move in words: waiting on you, with creator, with Fabulate team, approved. Overdue reviews surface in the Inbox under “Waiting on others” with how long they have waited.

For a decision

Questions to settle before build

01Should client notes always route through the Fabulate team, or can a brand on self-serve send them straight to the creator? The prototype assumes the team sits in between.
02Who on the client side can approve, and does an approval need one named approver or several? The prototype shows one approver with other client colleagues able to comment.
03How many revision rounds are included? This should come from the contract terms per engagement, so the room can flag a round beyond what was agreed rather than blocking it.
04Is a concept gate required on every engagement, or only where the contract names a concept deliverable? Some campaigns go straight to cut.
05What review turnaround do we commit to clients, so the Inbox can surface an overdue review against a real deadline?
